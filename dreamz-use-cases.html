Use cases

  • Perimenopause and postmenopause: When hormonal changes disrupt sleep

    Research links the hormonal changes women experience during the menopause transition with shifts in sleep, including more difficulty settling at night. During perimenopause, estrogen and progesterone begin to fluctuate and then decline. Declining estrogen is associated with a narrower comfortable temperature range in the body, which is linked to night sweats, hot flashes, and restless nights. Dreamz tracks overnight temperature, heart rate variability, and movement, so women can see how their nights vary.

    Dreamz helps women fall asleep faster and shows how their nights change over time, so they can spot patterns and bring real data to conversations with their providers.

  • Insomnia: When sleep takes too long to arrive

    For many people, the hardest part of the night is the stretch between lights out and sleep. Many people describe checking the time again and again while waiting for sleep, and research links that rising frustration with heightened arousal. Dreamz gives the brain a physiological assist at that moment.

    Dreamz shortens the stretch between lights out and sleep, so the night starts with rest.

  • Anxiety: When your mind won't quiet down

    Sleep research consistently shows that the brain needs to shift out of a high-arousal state before sleep can begin. Dreamz supports that shift directly, helping the brain settle so people fall asleep faster.

    With the mind settled sooner, the night starts calmer and sleep arrives faster.

  • PTSD: When trauma-related stress follows you to bed

    Research on sleep and emotional processing suggests that high-quality rest plays a meaningful role in how the brain handles stressful experiences, and many people living with trauma-related stress report difficulty falling asleep. Dreamz helps people fall asleep faster by acting on the physiological mechanisms behind sleep onset, quieting the mental noise that often keeps the mind active at bedtime.

    Getting to sleep sooner gives the brain more time in the restorative stages that research links to emotional regulation.
